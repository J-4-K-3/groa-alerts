# G.R.O.A. — Global Risk Observation and Analysis

**A global intelligence and public-safety platform by Innoxation Tech Inc.**

🌐 **Live platform:** https://gloa-alerts.vercel.app/

## About G.R.O.A.

G.R.O.A. is a free, open platform designed to provide timely information on global crises, risks, and relief efforts. It connects people with real-world organizations and communities, offering verified updates and situational awareness without barriers.

### Ownership & Development

G.R.O.A. is owned, developed, and funded by Innoxation Tech Inc., a technology company building diverse platforms for social connection, app distribution, and global safety.

We are growing and remain committed to transparency, accessibility, and community empowerment.

### How It Works

There are no accounts, no payments, and no barriers. Simply visit the platform, explore the information you need, send notes if you wish, report issues, and leave.

G.R.O.A. is free, convenient, and designed to give people more insight with less friction.

## The Science of Safety

G.R.O.A. is more than a map. It is a global intelligence network designed to support public safety through information gathering, evidence evaluation, and real-time situational awareness.

### Transparency

Information and alerts are supported by source intelligence and available evidence, helping users understand where information comes from and how it should be interpreted.

### Accuracy

Multi-layer information evaluation, source assessment, and verification workflows help distinguish reliable information from unconfirmed or conflicting reports.

### Timeliness

Automated data pipelines and intelligence processing are designed to help surface developing situations and relevant updates promptly.

### Human-Centric

G.R.O.A. is designed for people, not just data points. Its services aim to translate complex information into understandable situational awareness and useful safety guidance.

## The G.R.O.A. Engine

G.R.O.A. brings together information from multiple sources, including free and paid data providers. It processes, correlates, scores, and ranks incoming information to help produce intelligence reports and support its public-facing services.

Its broader architecture incorporates data collection, research, source intelligence, correlation, verification workflows, situational assessment, and human-reviewed safety operations.

Core technology concepts include:

- Machine-learning-assisted detection and analysis.
- Source reliability assessment and information ranking.
- Multi-source correlation and evidence evaluation.
- Distributed information collection and reporting.
- AI-assisted semantic analysis and intelligence summarization.
- Situation monitoring, threat indicators, and forecast scenarios.

The platform's outputs depend on available sources, evidence quality, processing status, and the capabilities enabled in the current implementation.

## Intelligence Modules

G.R.O.A. contains specialized internal modules that support different stages of the intelligence process.

### R.E.D. — Researched Emerging Data

Researches emerging information, correlates related material, summarizes findings, and supports follow-up research.

### V.I.R.U.S. — Verified Intelligence for Regional Urgency Situations

Evaluates individual and regional situations, assigns urgency classifications, and supports appropriate review and escalation pathways.

### S.I.I.T. — Situational Intelligence for Incoming Threats

Maintains current situation assessments, interprets incoming evidence, monitors threat indicators, and develops plausible future scenarios with explicit uncertainty.

### T.W.O W.E.E.K.S. — Threat Warning Operations for War Evacuations, Escalations and Keeping Safety

Supports human-reviewed warnings, escalation workflows, evacuation preparation, and the tracking of safety-related operations through authorized integrations.

These modules work within the wider G.R.O.A. architecture. Their responsibilities remain distinct so that research, urgency classification, situational assessment, and consequential response workflows are not treated as interchangeable functions.

## Evidence & Verification Philosophy

G.R.O.A. does not treat every source as proof, nor does it automatically consider information verified because it originates from a government, an official institution, a satellite provider, or another recognized source.

Sources provide information that must be evaluated in context. G.R.O.A. considers available evidence, source reliability, independent corroboration, conflicting reports, corrections, and the degree of uncertainty surrounding a developing situation.

Its verification process is designed to distinguish between reported claims, supporting evidence, corroborated information, disputed findings, and verified conclusions. Information that has not been sufficiently assessed must not be presented as though its accuracy has been established.

### Verification Tiers

- **Tier 1 — Government & Satellite Evidence:** Information supported by relevant government records or satellite-derived data, subject to evaluation and corroboration where appropriate. The source category alone does not establish that a claim is true.
- **Tier 2 — Multi-Source Independent Reports:** Information supported by multiple reports whose independence and relevance have been assessed. Repeated reporting from a shared origin does not automatically constitute independent corroboration.
- **Tier 3 — Developing Situation / Single Source:** Preliminary information that remains under investigation, has limited corroboration, or relies on a single reporting source.

These tiers describe the evidence and reporting context; they should not be interpreted as an unconditional guarantee of accuracy.

### Reducing Misinformation

G.R.O.A. aims to reduce misinformation through evidence evaluation, source assessment, information correlation, verification workflows, and the identification of conflicting or unsupported claims.

Misinformation cannot be eliminated completely. Information may be incomplete, inaccurate, manipulated, delayed, or subsequently corrected. G.R.O.A. therefore treats verification as an ongoing process and recognizes the importance of uncertainty, corrections, and reassessment.

The objective is to help people make better-informed decisions by providing greater transparency into what is known, what is supported by evidence, and what remains uncertain.
